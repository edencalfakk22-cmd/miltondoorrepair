FAQs

Thresholds & Weatherstripping — your questions

How long should a threshold and seals last on a warehouse entrance?

Noticeably less than the rest of the door. Compression seals flatten, sweeps abrade against grit, and a sill sitting in a salt path corrodes; on a high-cycle opening that is a few seasons rather than a decade. They are consumables, and treating them as consumables is far cheaper than heating a building through the gap they leave.

Will sealing the door actually cut our heating cost?

On a badly sealed entrance, noticeably, because the perimeter of a door is the largest continuous gap in that wall and a warehouse opening often has a pressure difference driving air through it. What sealing will not do is fix a leaf that is out of square: the gasket then crushes at one end and never touches at the other. Square first, seal second.

Meltwater keeps freezing under the door overnight. Can that be stopped?

Largely. What normally happens is that a worn sweep lets slush under the leaf during the day and it refreezes at night into a ridge the door then rides over. A new sweep and threshold, the right clearance, and clearing the drainage path in front of the sill takes most of it away. In a heavy freeze-thaw week nothing is perfect, but the door should not be frozen to its own sill in the morning.
