FAQs

Hollow Metal Dock & Service Door Repair — your questions

Forklifts keep hitting the same frame. Is there anything beyond repairing it again?

Yes, and on a repeat opening it is worth doing. Armour plate over the strike zone, pull hardware moved out of the traffic line, corner guards on the jamb, and a heavier hinge so repeated shock does not walk the screws loose. The impacts keep happening; they just land on parts that are cheap and quick to replace.

The bottom of our dock door has rusted out. Repair or replace?

Repair, if the stiles and lock edge are still solid. Salted meltwater sits in the bottom channel and eats the door from the inside, so the outer skin fails last and the damage is generally worse than it looks. We cut back to clean steel, weld in new material and fit a sweep. Once corrosion reaches the lock edge, or the door carries a fire label, the leaf gets replaced and we will tell you so.

Does a fire label survive a repair?

It depends entirely on what is done to the door. Field work on a labelled door and frame has real limits, and cutting into a leaf or changing the hardware prep can put the rating in question. We work within what is permitted, say plainly when a proposed repair would not be, and where the label cannot be preserved we tell you the opening needs a rated replacement rather than quietly repairing it anyway.

Our rear door was pried open. How fast can it be made secure?

Securing it is the first visit and usually the same day: the lock edge is straightened enough to latch, a temporary strike or latch guard goes on, and the building locks that night. The permanent repair — jamb re-anchored, strike area rebuilt, leaf re-hung — is booked separately, because doing that properly takes the opening out of service for a while.
