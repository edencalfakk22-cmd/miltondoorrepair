The failure is nearly always the same: the door drops at the lock corner, drags on the threshold, and the deadlatch stops finding the strike. It is rarely the lock. It is a worn bottom pivot, a stretched top corner, or a frame the building has pushed out of square.

We take the door out of the frame, replace the worn pivots and bearings, re-square the corners, reset the threshold and put the closer back to a sensible setting — then confirm it latches and locks unaided before we leave. FAQs

Storefront & Aluminum Door Repair — your questions

Why does my Milton storefront door drag on the threshold every winter?

Salt, sand and slush get tracked into the bottom pivot and grind it out, and freeze-thaw movement shifts the frame at the same time. The door then sits low at the lock corner and catches on the threshold. Replacing and repacking the pivot and re-squaring the door fixes it properly; adjusting the closer only hides it for a few weeks.

Can a bent aluminum storefront door be repaired, or does it need replacing?

Most can be repaired. We straighten and reinforce corners, replace stiles, rails and pivot hardware, and re-hang the door. We only recommend replacement when the extrusion itself is torn or the frame is beyond straightening — and we tell you which it is before any work starts.

Our unit fronts onto a busy Milton plaza and the door is used constantly. How often should it be serviced?

A high-traffic entrance is worth checking twice a year, ideally before winter and again in spring once salt season is over. Pivots, closers and deadlatches are wear items on a door cycling hundreds of times a day, and catching them early avoids the after-hours call when the door will not lock at closing.
