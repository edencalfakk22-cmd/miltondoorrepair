The failure is nearly always the same: the door drops at the lock corner, drags on the threshold, and the deadlatch stops finding the strike. It is rarely the lock. We take the door out of the frame, replace the worn pivots and bearings, re-square the corners, reset the threshold and put the closer back to a sensible setting — then confirm it latches and locks unaided before we leave. FAQs

Aluminum Storefront Entrance Repair — your questions

How much movement does it take before a door stops latching?

Less than people expect. A deadlatch engages over a few millimetres of throw, so a leaf that has dropped three or four millimetres at the lock corner is already catching the lip of the strike instead of entering it. That is why the fault feels sudden: nothing changes for two years, the pivot wears past tolerance, and the door stops locking inside a week.

Our plaza entrance is already on a service contract and still fails every winter. What is being missed?

Usually the pivot pocket and the seasonal setting. A visual check finds a loose arm and a dirty track; it does not find brine and grit packed into the bottom pocket, which is what grinds a new pivot out within a season. And a closer set on a mild day has no margin at minus fifteen. Cleaning and repacking the pocket, then setting sweep and latch speed for winter, is what changes the outcome.
