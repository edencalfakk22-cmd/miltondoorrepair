It sticks and it will not latch are the two calls we take most in Milton, and they are usually the same problem seen in two different seasons. Humid summers swell wood doors and older frames until they bind; a January cold snap contracts everything, the closer slows, and the deadlatch stops reaching the strike. In between, freeze-thaw keeps nudging the frame, so an opening that was fine last year is out by a few millimetres this year.

In the newer subdivisions there is a third cause on top of the weather: a house that is still settling. Doors in Ford, Cobban, Walker, Harrison and Coates commonly go out of square in their first couple of winters, and builder-grade hinges and strikes are the first things to let go. We diagnose the whole assembly — hinges, pivots, frame square, strike position, threshold height and closer settings — and correct the cause instead of moving the strike plate and hoping. On commercial doors we set the closer so it still latches when the fluid is cold and thick.

FAQs

Door Alignment & Latch Repair — your questions

Our house is two years old and every interior door has gone off. Is that a warranty issue?

It is settlement, and it is normal. A new house moves through its first couple of years, and builder-grade hinges and strikes have almost no tolerance built into them, so a frame that shifts a few millimetres puts every latch out. Whether it is covered is between you and the builder. Either way the repair is re-hanging the leaves and relocating the strikes, and a whole house is usually one visit.

The door closes but does not latch. Is that not simply the lock?

It is a position problem far more often than a lock problem. The latch bolt is fixed relative to the top of the leaf, so if the door sagged or the frame racked, the bolt now arrives high, low or short of the strike opening. Replacing the lock puts a brand-new bolt in exactly the same wrong place. We mark where it is landing, correct the door, then move the strike.

Does re-hanging a door actually last, or is it back in a year?

It lasts when the cause was addressed. Lifting a leaf on a worn pivot without cleaning the pocket, or squaring a door in a frame whose anchors have let go, buys a season. Renewing the wear part, clearing the abrasive out and re-anchoring the frame is what makes it hold — which is why we quote what the opening needs rather than the cheapest thing that makes the symptom disappear today.
